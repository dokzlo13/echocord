# EC-021 Picker clarity, queued-change readability and meter motion

Deferred follow-up after the completed UI iteration. Owner unassigned. Depends on EC-013, EC-018 and EC-019. This record schedules no immediate implementation or new design exploration.

## Evidence and intent

Post-implementation user testing confirms that the app and live meters work. Remaining usability observations concern destination hierarchy, identifying queued route edits, and abrupt meter movement. These are qualitative observations, not measured latency, frame-rate or long-session results. Preserve the accepted direction and working audio/control behavior.

## Bounded follow-ups

- **Destination picker:** make server, category and voice-channel hierarchy easier to scan. Investigate grouping, indentation, spacing and selected/pending markers. Test multiple servers, repeated channel names and search results without losing their parent context.
- **Queued changes:** make every changed route field identifiable, including its confirmed and proposed values. A compact vertical list of labelled entries is a candidate, replacing the dense single-line summary. Distinguish input changes from destination changes and include server context where room names alone are ambiguous. Preserve partial-apply/cancel truth and usable Apply/Discard actions.
- **Meter motion:** investigate observed jumps before choosing a remedy. Compare the existing roughly 10Hz observations, 20Hz presentation cap and 200ms native output-peak hold. Consider bounded attack/release smoothing or a justified cadence adjustment as separate options. A faster display loop alone cannot produce fresher measurements. Preserve truthful numeric levels, responsive mute/stop, null/stale handling and hidden-window pausing; do not synthesize measurement history or rerender the control surface.

## Acceptance for a later iteration

Review rendered picker and queued-change proposals before implementation. Keep confirmed routes, readings and core actions visible at the supported compact sizes without main-surface scrolling; selection lists may scroll inside drawers. Validate single and combined edits, duplicate room names across servers, search, cancellation and partial failure. Compare meter behavior with deterministic level steps, silence, mute and resumed visibility; check render isolation and resource cost if cadence changes. Native perceived smoothness needs a real-use check, separate from synthetic timing tests.

This work does not add capture modes, multiple-source mixing or destination fan-out. Those remain EC-017/020; scheduling priority between the follow-ups is a future decision.
